How to read the verdicts

  • Allowed an express grant — robots Allow or a no-permission-needed hyperlink policy — and nothing against automation
  • Allowed, with conditions nothing forbids fetching; the site's terms condition reuse (attribution, permission for republication) and those conditions are listed
  • Silent the host publishes no terms at all, or none that touch access; robots does not restrict our paths; the position rests on open publication and the RTI Act s.4 duty
  • Not allowed an express prohibition on automated access or a robots Disallow over our paths
  • Could not verify nothing on disk supports a position either way

Two questions are kept apart throughout, because they have different answers: access — may the tracker fetch the listing and read the document — and reuse — may the firm copy what it fetched. A reproduction condition (attribution, permission before republishing) is a condition on the optional internal PDF archive and on onward circulation, not a bar on fetching. Metadata — title, date, type, link — is fact and attracts no copyright (EBC v Modak).

Access — what the site says about fetching it

(a) Automated access, crawling, bots, rate limits, bulk download: SILENT on all three captured pages. Not one of them mentions robots, crawlers, spiders, scraping, harvesting, data mining, automated retrieval, systematic access or load. /disclaimer is an as-is, no-warranty, use-at-own-risk notice ("The use of website is at the User's sole risk", https://trai.gov.in/disclaimer) that also points users to the Gazette of India for the authoritative text of regulations and tariff orders. /terms-conditions is accuracy/liability boilerplate under Indian law and Indian-court jurisdiction ("should not be construed as a statement of law", https://trai.gov.in/terms-conditions) plus an external-links notice. /website-policy is the only page that speaks to how the site may be used: its Hyperlinking Policy affirmatively permits direct (deep) linking to hosted information with no prior permission, asks (not requires) to be informed of links, and forbids loading TRAI pages into frames on another site, requiring them to open in a new browser window. Its Privacy Policy discloses server-side logging of IP address, domain, browser, OS, visit time and pages visited, with no attempt to identify individuals unless an attempt to damage the site is detected. Nothing on any page conditions access on registration, login, human-only use, or a User-Agent.

Reuse — what it says about copying its content

(b) The Copyright Policy on https://trai.gov.in/website-policy says material "may be reproduced free of charge after taking proper permission by sending a mail" to TRAI; reproduction must be accurate, not derogatory or misleading; where material is published or issued to others the source must be prominently acknowledged; and the permission does not extend to any material identified as third-party copyright, for which the copyright holder must be approached. The footer on every page carries a general "Copyright (c) 2024 TRAI. All rights reserved" notice. /disclaimer and /terms-conditions contain no reuse or licence terms. This is a reproduction condition: it bears on the optional internal PDF archive and on any onward circulation of TRAI text, not on fetching a listing page or reading a PDF. The prior analysis's position stands: bibliographic metadata is fact (EBC v Modak) and outside the clause; gazetted regulations, tariff orders and directions are Official Gazette matter under Copyright Act s.52(1)(q)(i), which a website policy cannot curtail; non-gazetted CPs, recommendations, press releases and debarments rest on s.52(1)(a) fair dealing for research (ANI Media v OpenAI, interim) while kept internal; one courtesy email obtains the standing permission the policy invites.

Anything forbidding what the tracker does

None directed at fetching. The only prohibitive language on the captured pages is (1) the Hyperlinking Policy's statement that TRAI does not permit its pages to be loaded into frames on another site (a display/embedding restriction, irrelevant to a tracker that links out), and (2) the Copyright Policy's requirement of prior permission by email before reproducing material (a reuse condition on republication/archiving, not a bar on access). Neither page contains any express prohibition on automated access, retrieval, crawling or scraping, and no robots Disallow covers our paths.

Verdict — why

Access is allowed: the pages are silent on automated access, the Hyperlinking Policy expressly grants direct linking without prior permission, robots.txt allows every path we fetch with no Crawl-delay, and the tracker's identifying User-Agent, robots enforcement, ~1 req/s and human-triggered runs sit comfortably within IT Act ss.43/66 as the prior analysis reasoned. The conditions attach not to fetching but to the optional PDF archive and any onward use, because TRAI's Copyright Policy conditions reproduction on permission by email and prominent attribution, and its Hyperlinking Policy forbids framing. Metadata storage engages none of this; the archive is defensible under s.52(1)(q)(i) for gazetted instruments and fair dealing for the rest while kept internal, and a single courtesy email closes the residual exposure.

Conditions to keep

  • Keep any PDF archive strictly internal to the firm; do not republish, circulate to clients or productise TRAI text without first sending the permission email TRAI's Copyright Policy invites (reproduction is offered free of charge).
  • Send that one courtesy email now to obtain standing permission; it also covers the non-gazetted shelves (consultation papers, recommendations, press releases, debarments) that otherwise rely on fair dealing under an interim authority.
  • Prominently acknowledge TRAI as source on any onward use of archived material, and reproduce it accurately, never in a derogatory or misleading context.
  • Do not archive or redistribute any item TRAI marks as third-party copyright; that material is expressly outside TRAI's permission.
  • Link out to TRAI pages; never load them in frames or iframes in the tracker UI (express no-framing rule); optionally notify TRAI of the links as the policy requests.
  • For trai_open_consultations, trai_whats_new and trai_rss store metadata only; archive PDFs at the item's primary venue.
  • Continue to honour robots.txt, keep the honest identifying User-Agent, and stay at ~1 req/s with retries rather than tighter loops on trai_miscellaneous, whose host intermittently times out.
